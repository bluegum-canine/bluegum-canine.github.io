The dog who
bit to get his way

A two‑year‑old small dog, surrendered to be put down after biting his owner repeatedly and badly. An unfinished case, written up while it is still going.

{{CASES_NAV}}

Luke is about two years old and a small dog. His owner had been bitten repeatedly, and badly, and gave him up to be put down.

When he arrived, he lunged and snapped at the gloves the moment anyone reached into his crate. It took bite gloves just to get a lead on him. The first clip below is that dog. It is not pleasant to watch, and I have left it in on purpose, because a case study that starts with the dog already fixed is not telling you anything.

The first question: fear, or something else?

Aggression in a dog is a symptom, and the plan depends entirely on what is producing it. A frightened dog needs distance and a change in what people predict. A dog who has found that biting works needs something quite different. Treat one as the other and you make it worse, so the first job was assessment, not training.

The trouble with assessing a dog on arrival is that arrival is the one moment you cannot trust. A new environment, strangers, a crate: any dog looks bad. So I waited.

What changed after a few days

Over the first few days, Luke worked out that biting was not getting him what it got him at home. Nobody backed off. And once the strategy stopped paying, he relaxed, and the dog underneath came out.

That dog did not look afraid. He was not shrinking, freezing or trying to get away. He loves other dogs — and is quite inappropriate with them, pushy and over the top, with no manners at all. And with people, what I saw looked much more like a dog who had been getting his own way than one who was frightened.

The assessment so far: this is not fear‑based. It looks like a dog who was never taught how to be around people, or around other dogs, and whose behaviour escalated until it got him what he wanted.

How a growl turned into bites

The likely sequence runs like this. Luke does not want to be petted, so he moves away. At some point he cannot move away. He growls. If the growl is ignored, he bites. And the bite works: the hand goes. A dog who gets that result a few times does not need to start with the growl any more.

That is a dog with a missing education, not a bad one. Nobody gave him the people socialisation or the dog socialisation he needed while he was young, and nobody taught him that there are other ways to say no. It is the same missed window as Stevie, coming out in a very different way. Her answer to pressure was fear. His is to take control of it.

The plan

  1. 01

    Stop the strategy paying

    Calm, consistent handling that does not retreat when he snaps. This is most of what the first few days were: not a technique, just being a person biting does not work on.

  2. 02

    Exercise and structure

    A tired dog with a routine is a dog who can think. Proper exercise every day, and rules that are the same every time.

  3. 03

    Obedience

    Teaching him that doing what he is asked is what gets him what he wants, in place of pushing until people give in.

  4. 04

    Proper socialisation, with people and with dogs

    Careful, graded exposure to the things he missed, including learning some manners with other dogs.

Where he is now

Days in, Luke is walking nicely on a lead and choosing to settle in close to people, which is the opposite of the dog in the crate. That is real progress, and it happened fast.

It is also only the beginning, and I would be misleading you if I presented it as more.

What this case does not claim

Luke is not fixed, and he is not yet ready to be rehomed. I expect that to take a few more weeks. A dog who has bitten badly has a history, and the right home for him is one that knows it and is prepared to keep up the work.

The assessment is also still an assessment. It is what I see so far, and I will change it if he shows me something different. This page will be updated with where he ends up.

As the video says: he is not out of the woods yet, but he is getting a chance.
