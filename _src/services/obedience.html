Why it falls apart outside

Good in the kitchen
is only the start

Almost every owner I speak to says the same thing: he knows it at home. And he usually does. What he doesn’t know yet is that the same word means the same thing on the beach, at the gate, with a cat in the hedge and with you sitting down instead of standing up.

That is why obedience is built in stages, and why the middle stages are the ones people skip. Teaching a dog what a word means should take the longest, and is almost always rushed. Reinforcing it until it is a habit, not a favour. Proofing it, which means deliberately practising around the distractions that break it, a little at a time. Maintaining it, which is the part that belongs to you, for the rest of the dog’s life.

Skip proofing and you have a dog that is obedient right up until it matters.

What we work on

Four things that
buy a dog its freedom

Recall

Coming back first time, straight to you, without a detour past the interesting smell. We start close, where your dog can hardly get it wrong, and add distance and distraction only as fast as it keeps succeeding. A long line keeps it safe in the meantime, so the dog never learns that ignoring you is an option.

Settle

Going to a spot and staying there, calmly, until you say it’s over. It is the most useful thing a family dog can know: visitors at the door, dinner on the table, the vet’s waiting room. The dog learns to switch itself off, rather than being held back.

Loose lead

A walk you actually enjoy. Pulling is not a dog being dominant; it is a dog that has learned pulling gets it where it wants to go faster. Change what pulling earns and the lead goes slack.

Stopping on command

One word that stops your dog where it is — before the road, before the sheep, before the child with the ice cream. It is the one you hope you never need, and the one worth proofing hardest.

What it asks of you

  • The same rules from everyone in the house. Dogs cope well with “always” and “never”. They cope very badly with “sometimes”. If one person allows the sofa and another doesn’t, that is a people problem to sort out first
  • The same words, meaning the same thing. Written down and on the fridge, so the children and the visitors use them too
  • A few minutes, a few times a day. Short sessions that end while the dog is still keen beat an hour on Sunday
  • Watching what your dog learns on its own. Your dog is awake for most of the day and learning the whole time. The counter that sometimes has food on it teaches more than any session I run

Common questions

Is my dog too old? No. Older dogs learn perfectly well; they may simply have more habits to undo first.

How long will it take? It depends on the dog, the habits already there and how much practice happens between sessions. I will give you an honest estimate once I have met you both.

Can I do it in a class instead? Yes, if your dog copes around others — see group classes. One-to-one suits problems that happen in one place, or a dog that isn’t ready for a room full of dogs.
