The first few months

What your puppy is
learning when you’re not looking

A puppy is learning every waking minute. Most of what it learns in the first months isn’t anything you meant to teach: that whining opens the crate door, that the kitchen floor is as good a toilet as the garden, that grabbing a sleeve starts a game. None of that is a bad puppy. It is a clever one, learning what works.

Puppy training is mostly about arranging the house so the right things work and the wrong things don’t — long before anything that looks like obedience.

Socialisation, done properly

Not meeting everything, but meeting things well: people of every shape, in hats, with beards and walking sticks; calm, grown-up dogs; floors, stairs, the car, the hoover. The aim is a puppy that is curious rather than frightened. We reward it for choosing to go closer, and never push a shy puppy into something it isn’t ready for. Confidence it finds on its own lasts.

Toilet training

  • Out on waking, after sleeping, eating, drinking and playing — on a written routine that everyone in the house follows
  • A few minutes of play outside after it goes, before coming back in. Otherwise it learns that going ends the fun
  • Accidents cleaned with an enzyme or oxygen cleaner, not an ammonia-based one, which smells like an invitation
  • Expect proper bladder control somewhere around four to six months, sooner in some dogs and later in small breeds, with the odd setback during growth spurts. That isn’t failure

The crate, alone time and the nights

A crate is a bedroom, never a punishment, and it is only as big as the puppy needs. Meals and chews happen in it so it becomes a place the puppy likes. Short spells in it while you are still home stop it meaning “everyone is leaving”. And the door opens for quiet, never for crying — which is hard on night two, and much easier by night ten.

Mouthing and biting

All puppies explore with their mouths, and teething hurts. The job is teaching a soft mouth and giving it better things to chew, not stopping it outright. We also start the early foundations of “leave it” and “give”, which later save socks, dinners and arguments.

Two puppies at once?

Two puppies of the same age often grow up relying on each other instead of on you, and one can struggle badly when they are apart. It can be done well, but each puppy needs time and training on its own. Worth a conversation before the second one comes home, if you can.

The terms

Who I work with

  • Any dog, of any breed, size or history
  • Puppies from eight weeks, at home. Until their vaccinations are finished, puppies are trained at home only, never in public places
  • Residential training from six months of age
  • Group classes for dogs that can cope near other dogs, once vaccinations are finished. A dog that isn’t ready yet starts one-to-one
  • Older dogs are welcome — I will be honest at the assessment about what is realistic
  • Females in season can carry on with lessons at home, but cannot start residential training or come to a group class until the season is over
