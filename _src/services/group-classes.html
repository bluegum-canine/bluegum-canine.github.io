Why a class at all

Other dogs are the
point, not the problem

Plenty of dogs will sit beautifully in the kitchen and forget their own name in the park. That is not stubbornness. A dog learns in the place it is taught, and until it has practised somewhere busy, the kitchen is the only place it really knows the job.

A class is the cheapest way to fix that. Every week there are other dogs, other people, new smells and a bit of noise — exactly the things that fall apart on a real walk. Getting a recall to work with six other dogs in the room is what makes it work at the beach.

There is a quieter benefit too. Owners in a class keep each other going. You see that everyone’s dog has an off week, you see what works for somebody else, and there is a gentle nudge to do the homework when you know you are back in seven days.

How the six weeks run

Each week builds
on the last

What a class looks like

  • We start with how the week at home went, because that is where most of the learning actually happens
  • Something new is shown, then every dog works it — with me going round, not standing at the front
  • Each exercise gets a little harder as the dogs cope: more distance, more time, more distraction
  • You leave with a short piece of homework for the week, small enough to fit into a normal day

What you do between classes

A few minutes, a few times a day, beats one long session at the weekend. Dogs learn from everything that happens to them, not just the bits we call training, so the homework is mostly about the ordinary moments: going out the door, meeting someone on the road, coming in from the garden. Everyone in the house using the same words for the same things makes more difference than almost anything I can do in an hour.

Who a class suits

  • Young dogs and adolescents who have the basics at home but lose them outside
  • Rescue dogs settling in who are comfortable around other dogs
  • Families who want everyone taught the same way at the same time

A class is not the right start for a dog that lunges, freezes or panics near other dogs. Putting it in a room with six of them teaches it that its fears were right. That dog starts one-to-one, and a class becomes the goal rather than the first step. Not sure which yours is? Tell me about it and I will give you an honest answer.

Common questions

Can I bring the children? Yes, if they can be calm around the dogs. A dog that listens to one person only is only a third trained.

My dog is a bit excitable around other dogs — is that the same as reactive? Usually not. Pulling to say hello is what a class is for. Barking, lunging or hiding to make another dog go away is different, and is worth a conversation first.

What if we miss a week? I will tell you what we covered so you can catch up at home. The terms are below.
