Common problems — 11

Refuses to get
in the car

A dog that plants itself on the drive, or has to be lifted in, or travels badly enough that nobody bothers taking it anywhere. Out here that is a real limit on a dog's life — almost everywhere worth walking is a drive away.

What it looks like

Bracing at the boot. Slinking away when the keys come out. Drooling, whining or being sick within a mile. Barking at every passing lorry. Some dogs are fine once moving and hate the getting in; some load happily and fall apart on the road.

Which half of the journey the trouble sits in tells you a great deal, so it is the first thing I ask.

Rule out the physical first

A dog that has been sick in a car a few times may simply have travel sickness, and no amount of confidence-building fixes nausea. Young dogs often grow out of it; some do not, and there is genuinely effective veterinary help.

Equally, a dog that has started refusing a jump it used to make without thinking may be telling you something about a joint rather than about the car. Health before behaviour, every time. A sudden change with no obvious cause goes to your vet before it comes to me.

Why it usually happens

The maths of where the car goes. For a great many dogs the car has meant the vet, the groomer, and the kennels — and almost nothing else. The dog has drawn an entirely reasonable conclusion.

One bad journey. Sickness, a hard stop, a crate that slid. Dogs form these associations in a single event and are in no hurry to revise them.

The car is genuinely unpleasant. Slippery boot floor, nothing to brace against, engine noise underneath. A dog that cannot stand up steadily is being thrown about for the whole journey.

What actually works

Uncouple the car from the destination. Sit in a parked car with the engine off and feed the dog dinner there. Do it for a week without going anywhere. This one step does more than everything else on this page combined.

Then very short journeys to good places. Two minutes to a field the dog likes, not forty minutes to the vet. The ratio of good destinations to bad ones has to change.

Fix the footing. A non-slip mat, and a crate or a harness so the dog is secure and not bracing on every bend. A dog that feels stable is a different passenger.

Never wrestle the dog in. Every forced loading confirms that the car is a thing that happens to it. It is also how people get bitten by dogs that have never bitten anyone.

Break the loading into pieces — near the car, a paw on the sill, front feet in, in and straight out again — and pay each piece properly.

One legal note

An unrestrained dog in a car is a hazard to itself and to you, and in a collision it becomes a projectile. A crate or a proper travel harness is worth having on those grounds alone, quite apart from the training.

A dog that travels well gets taken places, and a dog that gets taken places has a considerably better life.
