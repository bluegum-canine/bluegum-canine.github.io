Poor recall

A dog you dare not let off the lead. Of everything on this list, this is the one that costs a dog the most, because a dog that cannot be let off spends its whole life on six feet of rope.

What it looks like

A dog that comes back beautifully in the garden and ignores you entirely in a field. A dog that comes when there is nothing better on offer. The long line that never comes off. Or the specific, heart-stopping version: a dog that hears you perfectly well, looks directly at you, and carries on.

Why it happens

Recall is not one behaviour, it is a competition. Every time you call, your dog weighs what you are offering against what the environment is offering. Out in a field the environment is offering a great deal — scent, movement, other dogs, possibly a rabbit — and in moments of high arousal that reward can vastly outweigh a piece of cheese in your pocket. High arousal is itself rewarding, which is why a dog mid-chase is not so much disobeying as unreachable.

There are three reliable ways a recall gets destroyed, and most dogs have had all three:

Calling and then ending the fun. If “come” predicts the lead going on and the walk finishing, the dog learns exactly what the word means and starts declining the invitation.

Calling when it will not work. Every call the dog ignores teaches it that the word is optional. Repeat that a few dozen times and the cue is worn out.

Telling the dog off when it eventually arrives. Understandable, and disastrous. From the dog’s side, the thing it just got punished for was coming back.

Why the usual fixes stall

The standard advice is better treats, and it does help in the early stage — but food alone cannot beat a rabbit, and it is not supposed to. Rewarding every single recall also builds the fastest, most fragile version of the behaviour there is. The dog that recalls brilliantly for six weeks and then stops is not being difficult; it is behaving exactly as the reward schedule taught it to.

A word on safety

Until the recall is genuinely proofed, use a long line, especially anywhere near stock or a road. There is no prize for testing an unfinished recall, and a single bad outcome near sheep is not recoverable — see livestock worrying.

A reliable recall is the single biggest expansion of a dog’s life you can buy it.
