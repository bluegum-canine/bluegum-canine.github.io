Case studies

The work,
written up

Every case I take gets written up properly: what the dog was doing, what was actually causing it, what we did about it, how long it really took, and where the dog ended up — including the parts that did not go to plan.

This is unusual, and it is deliberate. Almost nobody in this trade publishes outcomes. You will find testimonials everywhere — a sentence of praise with a first name attached — and almost no record of what was done or how long it really took.

Testimonials tell you a client was pleased. A case study lets you judge the work.

It is also what Sharon and I would want to read before letting a stranger near one of our own dogs, which is the test I am holding this page to. There are two cases so far, both labelled honestly for what they are. There will be more from January, written the same way.

The cases so far

  1. 01

    Chloe: The dog with nothing to pay her with

    A stray Doodle with a bite history, who would not take food and would not play. Seven days to a reliable off‑lead recall with people and dogs in the field.

  2. 02

    Stevie: The window that closed too early

    A two‑year‑old Great Dane who had never been properly socialised, and who had learned that a growl makes people go away. Several weeks of desensitisation — most of it done by her owner, not by me.

I trained Chloe and Stevie at Highland Canine in North Carolina. I assessed both dogs, decided what to do with them and did the work. Bluegum Canine will start work in Ireland from January 2027, and until then I would rather show you two honest training cases than fill this page with somebody else’s dog or a story I have quietly improved.

When those cases do arrive, they will be written up at our own kitchen table, about dogs we have had in our own house or gone out to in yours.

From January, every write-up here will carry:

  1. 01

    The presenting problem

    What the owner rang about, in their words.

  2. 02

    What was actually going on

    The assessment, the specific trigger, and what the behaviour had been getting the dog.

  3. 03

    The plan

    What we did, in what order, and why that order.

  4. 04

    How long it took

    Real elapsed time, including the setbacks. Not the version where everything goes smoothly.

  5. 05

    Where the dog ended up

    Honestly. Some cases are managed rather than resolved, and saying so is the whole point of writing them up.

Nothing goes on this page without the owner’s written permission, and names are changed unless they ask otherwise. Photography, video and recording at Highland Canine is done with the school’s express permission.
