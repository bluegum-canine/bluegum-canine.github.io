How I can help

Prices are on this page because you should not have to ring a stranger to find out whether you can afford them. Payment, cancellations and refunds are on the policies page.

Four ways in, each with its own page. Which one suits you depends less on the problem than on how much of the work you want to do yourself.

Twenty per cent off, for my first six months

Everything on this page is twenty per cent less than the published price for the first six months after I open. That includes the assessment visit.

I am not doing it to undercut anybody. I am doing it because I want the first dogs through the door to be the right ones, and because a trainer with no local case studies behind him is asking you to take his word for it. The prices go back to the list below when the six months are up. They do not creep back, and there is no sales pitch attached to that.

Every figure in the right-hand column is the usual price less twenty per cent. It is the price of the thing itself — one session, one course, one stay — not a charge for the six months. Everyone starts the same way: an assessment visit at your home. An hour or so, the dog in its own surroundings, and an honest recommendation at the end. It is deducted from whatever follows — a block of six one-to-ones, a behaviour programme, puppy foundations, residential training. How it works

Four steps
to getting started

  1. 01

    We talk first, and it costs nothing

    A phone call, no obligation. I ask a great many questions and you get a plain answer about whether this is something I can help with. If it is not, I will say so and point you at who can — I am not going to drive across three counties to tell you that on your doorstep. A video helps, if you can manage one. Footage of the dog actually doing it tells me more than any description can.

  2. 02

    The assessment visit — €150, or €120 with the launch discount

    If it sounds like something I can take on, I come to you. Sixty to ninety minutes, sometimes less, sometimes longer if the dog needs it. I see the dog at home and in the places the behaviour actually happens, because a dog in its own kitchen shows you things a dog in a training hall never will. At the end you get a straight recommendation: one-to-one sessions, a behaviour programme, or residential training.

  3. 03

    What you paid for the visit comes off what we do next

    Not a notional discount — the actual figure, off the actual bill. The assessment is not an extra fee bolted on the front; it is the first part of the work. The one exception is a single one-to-one session, which costs exactly what the visit costs, so there would be nothing left of it. It comes off a block of six, and off everything else.

  4. 04

    You get a written plan, and support while you work it

    Nobody remembers a training session three weeks later. Written down, it survives.
