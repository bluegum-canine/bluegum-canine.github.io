Common problems — 09

Barking at
every visitor

The doorbell goes and the house erupts. Five minutes of noise before anyone can hear themselves speak, and a dog that has to be shut in the kitchen every time somebody calls.

What it looks like

Barking at the bell, at a knock, at a car on the gravel, at the postman at the same time every morning. Some dogs settle once the visitor is in and sitting down. Some do not settle at all. Some are perfectly friendly the whole time and simply extremely loud about it.

Why it is so hard to shift

Because from the dog's side, it works every single time.

The postman arrives. The dog barks. The postman leaves. From the dog's point of view that is a job well done, repeated daily, for years. This is the most reliably reinforced behaviour in most households and nobody ever intended to train it.

Add to that the fact that barking at an arrival is genuinely a dog-shaped thing to do. We are not correcting a fault so much as renegotiating a habit the dog considers useful.

Work out which one you have

Alerting. Something happened and the dog is announcing it. Usually stops once the situation is resolved and the visitor is in.

Excitement. Visitors are wonderful and the dog cannot contain it. Often accompanied by jumping and a great deal of movement.

Fear. The dog does not want the visitor to come in and is saying so. This one gets worse if it is punished, and it is more common than owners expect — a dog can be delighted to see you and genuinely worried about a stranger in the hall.

The three look similar in the first ten seconds and need different plans, which is why the first thing I do is watch an arrival.

Why shouting does not help

To a dog that is already barking, a person raising their voice is a person joining in. Whatever you meant, what arrived was noise at the same moment as the trigger — and in an anxious dog, that makes the arrival more alarming rather than less.

The aim is not a silent dog. It is a dog that tells you somebody is here and then lets you deal with it.
