The method

Two principles

01

Freedom through obedience

A dog that will recall, settle and walk on a loose lead is a dog that gets to come with you. Obedience is not about control — it is what buys a dog its liberty.

02

The faster they learn,
the faster they forget

Behaviour built on constant reward is acquired fastest and lost fastest. Reliability comes from thinning the reward and proofing the behaviour — not from more treats.

A balanced assessment and training approach, built for your dog rather than its breed.

No two dogs arrive with the same history of what has paid off for them, so the plan is built for the individual. Correction is reserved for behaviours with real safety consequences; nuisance habits are better solved by changing the setup. More on how I work →

Where most people start

If you’ve already tried

Most of the dogs I see are not a first attempt. Their owners have read the books, watched the videos, and very often worked with a trainer already.

That is a good place to start from — it usually means the groundwork is there and something specific is missing. Advice that works beautifully for one dog can do very little for another.

Pulling on the lead

Walks that are a battle, not a pleasure.

Nervous around people

Shrinking, barking or hiding when visitors call.

The velcro dog

Follows you room to room, cannot settle alone.

Poor recall

A dog you dare not let off the lead.

Destructive at home

Chewing, digging and damage while you are out.

Livestock worrying

The most serious problem a dog can have out here.

Every one of these is workable. And no dog is too old to learn.

Learning does not stop with age. Older dogs take on new behaviour perfectly well, given the steps in order.

The stepped approach

Four steps

Every behaviour goes through the same four steps. Skip one and it will not hold.

  1. 01

    Teaching

    What the behaviour is. Should take the longest; most often rushed.

  2. 02

    Reinforcing

    Consistent consequences, until it is reliable rather than optional.

  3. 03

    Proofing

    Distraction and distance, until it holds in a three-ring circus.

  4. 04

    Maintaining

    Keeping it there. This is where I teach you, not the dog.
